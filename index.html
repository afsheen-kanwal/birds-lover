    • European Robin

    The European Robin (Erithacus rubecula) is a small, insectivorous passerine bird easily recognized by its distinctive bright orange-red breast and face. Common across Europe, western Asia, and parts of North Africa, it is known for its curious nature and melodious, year-round song. It frequently visits gardens, parks, and woodlands, often following gardeners to feed on unearthed earthworms and insects.

    Indian Peafowl

    The Indian Peafowl (Pavo cristatus), specifically the male peacock, is celebrated worldwide for its spectacular and colorful courtship display. Native to the Indian subcontinent, the male features an iridescent blue-green neck and an elongated train of upper tail coverts adorned with brilliant eye-spots (ocelli). They use this massive, fans-shaped train to attract peahens during the breeding season.

    Peregrine Falcon

    The Peregrine Falcon (Falco peregrinus) is a legendary bird of prey renowned as the fastest creature in the animal kingdom, capable of reaching diving speeds over 320 km/h (200 mph). It features a bluish-grey back, a barred white underbody, and a distinctive black mustache-like marking on its face. It has a global distribution and is highly adapted to hunting other birds mid-air.

    Bee-eaters

    Bee-eaters are a family of brightly colored, slender birds characterized by richly tinted plumage, long down-curved bills, and elongated central tail feathers. True to their name, their diet primarily consists of flying insects, especially bees and wasps. They catch their prey on the wing and safely remove stings by repeatedly beating the insect against a hard surface before consuming it.

    Gouldian Finch

    The Gouldian Finch (Chloebia gouldiae), also known as the rainbow finch, is a stunningly colorful passerine bird endemic to northern Australia. Exhibiting bright combinations of green, yellow, purple, blue, and red feathers, these small finches inhabit open tropical savannah woodlands. Due to their striking appearance, they are highly popular in aviculture but remain threatened in the wild.

    Bald Eagle

    The Bald Eagle (Haliaeetus leucocephalus) is a majestic bird of prey and the national symbol of the United States. Instantly recognizable by its snowy-white head, dark brown body, and powerful yellow beak and talons, this large raptor builds the largest tree nests of any North American bird. It thrives near large bodies of open water where fish are abundant.t

    Zosterops palpebrosus

    The Oriental White-eye (Zosterops palpebrosus) is a tiny passerine bird widespread throughout the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia. It is easily identified by its yellowish-green upperparts, pale underparts, and a very prominent, distinctive ring of tiny white feathers around each eye. They are highly active foragers, moving in small flocks through gardens and canopies to feed on insects and nectar.

    Scarlet Robin

    The Scarlet Robin (Petroica boodang) is a small, brightly colored bird native to continental Australia and Tasmania. The male features a striking bright red chest, contrasting sharply with its black head, back, and wings, and a prominent white patch above its beak. They are quiet and observant predators, typically perch-hunting for small invertebrates in open forests and woodlands.
